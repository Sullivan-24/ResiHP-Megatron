# Evaluation

Everything below is regenerated by `resihp-bench all` (outputs under `results/`). Three kinds
of evidence, deliberately kept apart:

1. **Measured on real processes** (CPU, gloo, 8 workers on one host): correctness and the MTTR
   *breakdown* of the control plane and runtime.
2. **Predicted by the planner + pipeline simulator** (the same code that produces executed
   schedules): degraded throughput of re-planned layouts.
3. **Simulated at cluster scale** (trace-driven, 1K-64K GPUs): ETTR of ResiHP against models of
   published systems, with every assumption exposed as a parameter.

GPU measurements are not part of this repository yet (see the last section).

## 1. Correctness

Every distributed test compares against single-process training of the same model in
float64 (`resihp/runtime/reference_model.py`, `resihp/runtime/moe.py`) and requires equal
losses and parameters to 1e-10 relative, *after* live reconfigurations:

| feature | test |
|---|---|
| heterogeneous TP (2/1/1/3), uneven PP, micro-batch migration, SP on/off | `tests/dist/test_hetero_runtime.py` |
| + zero-bubble (B/W split), + interleaved VPP, + context parallelism | same file |
| kill in a TP group -> replan -> TP reshard + layer moves + replica recovery (also VPP, CP) | `tests/dist/test_resilient_hetero.py` |
| FP8-style amax history migrated and rolled back (kill after the amax update) | same file |
| fail-slow detected, localised, mitigated by replan | `tests/dist/test_failslow_e2e.py` |
| replacement node rejoins and receives state; leader failover; leader dies mid-transaction | `tests/dist/test_rejoin_failover.py` |
| EP rank loss -> expert re-placement with replicas, expert + router-bias state migration | `tests/dist/test_moe_ep.py` |
| two `kill -9` without checkpoints; resharding checkpoint restore; snapshot replication | `tests/dist/test_resilient_dp.py` |

A mutation that removes the buffer rollback makes the FP8 test fail (checked while developing).

## 2. Chaos: MTTR breakdown on real processes (`results/chaos/chaos.md`)

8 workers (2 replicas x 2 stages x TP2) on 2 simulated nodes; faults injected from outside at
iteration 8 (20 for `slow`, after the detector's warm-up); medians of 3 trials.

| fault | recovered | detect | abort | plan | build + migrate | commit | fault -> commit | commit -> 2 iterations | semantics |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `kill -9` | 3/3 | 0.01 s | 0.01 s | 0.006 s | 0.03 s | 0.011 s | 0.07 s | 0.21 s | exact |
| SIGSTOP hang | 3/3 | 2.98 s | 0.02 s | 0.009 s | 0.04 s | 0.005 s | 3.06 s | 7.28 s | exact |
| 2x fail-slow (duty-cycled SIGSTOP) | 3/3 | 0.18 s | 0.06 s | 0.005 s | 0.05 s | 0.007 s | 0.31 s | 0.00 s | exact |

* Hang detection is bounded by the worker heartbeat timeout (3 s here).
* `commit -> 2 iterations` after a hang (~7 s) is the gloo harness: an operation already blocked
  on the frozen peer cannot be aborted and returns only at the 10 s communication timeout. NCCL
  abort removes this on GPUs; it is reported, not hidden.
* Absolute times are tiny because the model is; the transfer and build terms scale with state
  size and are modelled in the cluster simulation (`resihp.plan.reconfig_cost`).

## 3. Planner ablation (`results/planner/ablation.md`)

LLaMA-2-13B-like model, TP8 x PP4, 16 micro-batches per replica; predicted throughput relative
to the healthy plan.

| GPUs | scenario | ResiHP | no TP shrink | no repartition | no migration | replica drop |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | 1 GPU lost | 0.899 | 0.898 | 0.800 | 0.898 | 0.566 |
| 64 | 2 GPUs lost (one per replica, same stage) | **0.910** | **stops** | 0.568 | 0.910 | 0.317 |
| 64 | node lost | 0.898 | 0.898 | 0.596 | 0.898 | 0.566 |
| 64 | 1 GPU 2x slow | 0.899 | 0.898 | 0.800 | 0.898 | 0.500 |
| 128 | 1 GPU lost | **0.915** | 0.873 | 0.900 | 0.915 | 0.758 |
| 128 | 2 GPUs lost | **0.871** | 0.854 | 0.790 | 0.821 | 0.698 |
| 128 | node lost | 0.873 | 0.873 | 0.795 | 0.873 | 0.758 |
| 128 | 1 GPU 2x slow | 0.915 | 0.915 | 0.900 | 0.915 | 0.500 |

Reading: layer repartition is the largest single contributor; TP shrinking matters when
eviction is impossible (both replicas of a stage hit: exclusion stops training) or expensive
(128 GPUs: +4 points); progress-aware migration matters with several weakened groups (+5
points). While building this table we found and fixed a simulator bug (1F1B in-flight limits
were not scaled by a group's share after migration, starving groups that absorb evicted work);
with the fix, evict-and-repartition is often as good as keeping a shrunk group, and the planner's
joint keep-or-evict decision picks whichever is predicted faster.

## 5. Cluster-scale simulation (`results/sim/cluster.md`)

**Workload.** 405B-class dense model, TP8 x PP16 (128 GPUs per replica), 16 micro-batches per
replica (Llama 3's 16M-token batch of 8K sequences on 16K GPUs), 10 s iterations, 30 days,
3 seeds.

**Failures.** Llama 3 405B pre-training statistics (419 unexpected interruptions in 54 days on
16,384 GPUs, 18 root-cause categories mapped to GPU / node / network / job-wide domains), as
per-GPU Poisson rates. Repair times (median 24 h GPU, 6 h node, 2 h network, lognormal) and
fail-slow statistics (rate 2x the GPU fail-stop rate, median 1.6x for a median 30 min, heavy
tail) are **assumptions** — the paper does not report them — and are swept below.

**Policies.** ResiHP (detect 3 s, communicator rebuild 10 s, migration 5 s, one warm-up iteration;
job-wide failures restart in place from T1 snapshots, 120 s + <= 5 iterations); `torchft`
(replica drop, global batch shrinks) and `torchft-bp` (batch preserved); NVRx (checkpoint
restart: detect 60 s, restart 300 s, checkpoints every 20 min, straggler exclusion after 5 min);
ReCycle (stage rerouting without TP shrink, no fail-slow handling); Oobleck (node-granular
re-instantiation, 60 s); Greyhound (fail-stop by restart, fail-slow by rebalancing after 10
iterations); Ideal (no overhead, perfectly elastic). Batch-preserving policies move whole
micro-batches (integer assignment). NVRx's defaults reproduce Llama 3's reported >90% effective
training time on 16K GPUs (91.8% with spares, fail-stop only).

**Calibration.** ResiHP's degraded throughput uses an analytic stage-capacity model whose
repartition blend and overhead are fitted to the real planner at DP 2/4/8
(`results/sim/calibration.md`: beta 0.15, overhead 0.4, RMSE 0.042).

ETTR, fail-stop only:

| policy | spares | 1K | 4K | 16K | 64K |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ResiHP | 0% | 99.4% | 98.1% | 97.3% | 95.4% |
| torchft | 0% | 96.5% | 94.4% | 92.5% | 88.2% |
| torchft-bp | 0% | 95.8% | 91.8% | 89.9% | 85.6% |
| ReCycle | 0% | 95.9% | 94.3% | 92.6% | 88.3% |
| Oobleck | 0% | 98.4% | 95.1% | 92.5% | 86.3% |
| NVRx | 0% | 95.7% | 93.2% | 86.4% | 66.7% |
| ResiHP | 2% | 100.0% | 99.8% | 99.4% | 97.6% |
| torchft / ReCycle | 2% | 99.9% | 99.6% | 98.4% | 93.8% |
| Oobleck | 2% | 99.9% | 99.4% | 97.7% | 91.2% |
| NVRx | 2% | 99.5% | 97.9% | 91.8% | 70.8% |

ETTR with fail-slows (2% spares): ResiHP 99.9 / 99.4 / 98.0 / 94.1%; Greyhound 99.3 / 96.8 /
88.7 / 65.9%; torchft 99.2 / 93.1 / 77.6 / 51.3%; NVRx 98.5 / 94.4 / 80.3 / 44.4%.

At 16K GPUs without spares the losses decompose as (downtime / rollback / degraded):
ResiHP 0.48 / 0.10 / 2.12%, torchft 0.71 / 0.85 / 5.91%, NVRx 3.19 / 4.77 / 5.69%.

**Sensitivity (16K GPUs, with fail-slows, no spares).** ResiHP stays at 95.0-97.3% across
restart times of 5-30 min, GPU repair medians of 6-72 h, 0.5-4x failure rates and 0-4x fail-slow
rates; torchft-style systems fall from 92.7% (no fail-slows) to 52.6% (4x fail-slows), NVRx from
77.5% to 52.0% as restart time grows from 5 to 30 min.

**How to read this honestly.**

* With spare nodes and fail-stop only, the gap to online replica-level systems is about one
  ETTR point at 16K GPUs (MTTR and rollback) and grows with scale (64K: 97.6 vs 93.8%).
* The large gaps come from (a) no spares — a dead GPU idles a whole 128-GPU replica in
  replica-granular systems — and (b) fail-slows, whose rates are assumed; the fail-slow-free
  columns are the conservative headline.
* Policy models are simplifications of the published systems (no artifact runs); their
  parameters are in `resihp/sim/cluster.py::baseline_params` and can be re-tuned.
